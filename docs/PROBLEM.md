# Problem framing

## Observation

African cities are increasingly diverse. People from different ethnic, religious, cultural and socioeconomic backgrounds increasingly share the same neighbourhoods, workplaces and public spaces.

They do not always share the same unwritten assumptions about everyday life.

A behaviour that seems ordinary to one person may be interpreted very differently by another. Examples can include use of shared resources, greetings and forms of address, privacy, visitors, noise, parking, waste, religious practices and boundaries.

The issue is not that people are different. The issue is that people often have no practical way to understand the assumptions behind those differences.

## Working problem statement

> People in diverse urban communities often lack accessible, trustworthy and locally relevant information that can help them understand different social expectations, navigate everyday friction and prevent misunderstandings from becoming conflict.

## Why it matters

Small misunderstandings can become repeated sources of mistrust. Community leaders may only see a problem after relationships have already deteriorated.

Before You Assume explores whether timely, local and practical information can help people pause before judging, communicate more clearly and resolve everyday friction earlier.
